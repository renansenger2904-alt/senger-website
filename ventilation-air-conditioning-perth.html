02The system

A kitchen exhaust is a system, not a fan.

Most problems on site come from designing one part at a time. I design the whole loop so the numbers agree with each other.

  1. Roof discharge. Vertical, at velocity, and clear of air intakes, openings and boundaries, so neighbours and your own supply fan don't breathe it.
  2. Exhaust fan and grease duct. Duty set from the real duct route: straight runs, bends, filters and the exit, not a guess off a catalogue.
  3. Canopy. Sized to the appliances under it, their type and rating, with the overhang and mounting height the Standard asks for.
  4. Make-up air. Every litre extracted has to come back in. Too little and doors whistle, burners starve and the hood stops capturing.
  5. Room balance and comfort. Supply placed so it doesn't blow across the hood, and a check on heat, because cooking lines and refrigeration condensers heat the room fast.
  6. Interlocks. No airflow, no gas. The control logic between fans, gas valve and reset is written down, so the electrician and gasfitter wire the same thing.
