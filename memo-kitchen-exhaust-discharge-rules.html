Where kitchen exhaust is allowed to discharge

Does kitchen exhaust have to discharge 3 m above the roof? A short answer with the clause behind it, for owners, builders and contractors in Western Australia.

By Renan Senger, mechanical engineer (MIEAust)
AS 1668.2-2012, the edition NCC 2022 calls up
3 min read
Short answer. Not under AS 1668.2-2012. The 3 m in Clause 3.10.3(b) is written for Type A effluent, and kitchen exhaust is Type B. Type B above 1000 L/s must discharge vertically at 5 m/s or more and sit at least 6 m from property boundaries, street boundaries, outdoor air intakes and natural ventilation openings. Several WA councils do impose 3 m in their own guidelines, so check the council that approves the job.

Above and below 1000 L/s

Kitchen exhaust becomes objectionable above 1000 L/s (Table 3.3), and outlets within a single 6 m radius are counted together for that flow. So a grease fan, a condensate fan and two oven vents on the same patch of roof are one discharge for classification, even though each has its own terminal.

Flow of the discharge groupWhat applies
Above 1000 L/sVertical, 5 m/s or more at the outlet, and 6 m from boundaries, intakes and openings (Cl 3.10.3)
Below 1000 L/sSeparation from Table 3.4, growing with flow: 1 m under 200 L/s, 2 m under 400, 3 m under 600, 4 m under 800, 5 m under 1000

The velocity is at the outlet, not in the duct

The 5 m/s is measured at the outlet or terminal area. A duct running at 8 m/s tells you nothing if the fan outlet is larger than the duct: the maximum outlet area is the airflow divided by 5. Size terminals for about 6 m/s so a 10 % shortfall in airflow still passes on the day it is commissioned.

Where the 3 m comes from

Council food-business guidelines. Cockburn, Wanneroo, Gosnells, Victoria Park, Melville and Nedlands all write a height into their documents, usually 3 m above a flat roof or 1 m above the ridge, some with a relaxation more than 15 m from any higher structure. Other councils simply refer to AS 1668.2 and set no height at all. Read the guideline of the council that will approve your job, not a neighbouring one.

What this changes on site

  • A stack costs sheet metal, stays, a support frame and a wind and roof check. Where no guideline requires it, that money buys nothing.
  • Spreading outlets apart to dodge the grouping rarely works: the grease fan alone is usually over 1000 L/s.
  • The cheaper fix is almost always moving the intake, not the outlets.

Questions

Does a commercial kitchen exhaust have to discharge 3 m above the roof in WA?

Not under AS 1668.2-2012. Clause 3.10.3(b) applies the 3 m to Type A effluent and kitchen exhaust is Type B. Above 1000 L/s, Type B must discharge vertically at 5 m/s or more and sit at least 6 m from boundaries, air intakes and natural ventilation openings. Cockburn, Wanneroo, Gosnells, Victoria Park, Melville and Nedlands impose a height in their own guidelines, so check the approving council.

How far must a kitchen exhaust outlet be from an air intake?

Six metres once the discharge group exceeds 1000 L/s. Below that, AS 1668.2-2012 Table 3.4 scales the separation with flow: 1 m under 200 L/s, 2 m under 400, 3 m under 600, 4 m under 800 and 5 m under 1000. Outlets within a single 6 m radius are added together to decide which case applies.
