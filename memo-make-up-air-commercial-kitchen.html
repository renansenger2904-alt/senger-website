Make-up air: how much, and where

How much make-up air does a commercial kitchen need? A short answer with the clause behind it, for owners, builders and contractors in Western Australia.

By Renan Senger, mechanical engineer (MIEAust)
AS 1668.2-2012, the edition NCC 2022 calls up
3 min read
Short answer. Every litre the hoods extract has to come back in. AS 1668.2-2012 does not set a percentage: Clause 3.8.3 requires make-up air to be supplied directly when the outdoor air already reaching the kitchen cannot offset the hood exhaust, Clause 3.8.4 caps the pressure drop across transfer openings at 12 Pa, and Clause 3.8.5 requires the supply fan to run whenever the exhaust runs. In an open-plan tenancy we normally supply about 90 % mechanically and let the rest transfer.

What the Standard says, and what is judgement

  • Clause 3.8.3 is a sufficiency test, not a percentage. If the outdoor air already supplied to the kitchen or the space next door cannot offset the hoods, dedicated make-up air is required.
  • Clause 3.8.4 sizes the transfer openings so the pressure difference between spaces stays at or below 12 Pa. Designing to about 5 Pa leaves margin for filters loading up.
  • Clause 3.8.5 interlocks the supply with the exhaust. No supply, no exhaust.
  • The 90 % is judgement. Keeping the kitchen slightly negative holds odour in. Take too much and doors get heavy, burners starve and the hood stops capturing.

Where the air enters matters as much as how much

Clause 3.3.2.1 requires supply outlets to be arranged so they do not reduce hood performance or cause excessive cross-draughts over the appliances. In practice that means diffusers on the far side of the kitchen, blowing down at low face velocity. A grille aimed at the front of a canopy pushes the plume straight out of the hood, and it is one of the most common faults we find in an existing fit-out.

Do not forget the gas

Where open flued gas appliances share the room, the products of combustion leave through their flues, and that air has to be counted in the balance too. If the mechanical supply is also the combustion air pathway, AS/NZS 5601.1:2022 requires it at low level and interlocked with the gas. See gas ovens: ventilation air and interlocks.

Tempered or not

Untempered filtered supply is the Perth baseline and it is what most budgets carry. In a hot kitchen, bringing 2000 L/s of summer air in untreated can undo the cooling you paid for, so the options are evaporative cooling, which adds moisture and maintenance, or refrigerated make-up air, which adds cost. That choice belongs in the heat load conversation, not in the exhaust one.
