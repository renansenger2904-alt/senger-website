Gas ovens: ventilation air and interlocks

Do gas ovens change the ventilation design? A short answer with the clause behind it, for owners, builders and contractors in Western Australia.

By Renan Senger, mechanical engineer (MIEAust)
AS 1668.2-2012, the edition NCC 2022 calls up
3 min read
Short answer. Yes. For open flued appliances in a room, AS/NZS 5601.1:2022 Clause 6.4.6.3.1 compares gas input to room volume: above 0.4 MJ/h per m³, additional ventilation is required. It can be a permanent opening direct to outside sized at 450 mm² per MJ/h, or a mechanical supply delivered at low level and interlocked so the gas shuts off if the air fails.

The number people get wrong

The figure quoted in the trade is often 3 MJ/h per m³. That is the limit for flueless appliances. A rack oven that takes its combustion air from the room and sends the products up a flue is open flued, and the trigger is 0.4 MJ/h per m³. Used by mistake, the wrong figure says no extra ventilation is needed at all.

The two pathways

PathwayWhat it requires
NaturalPermanent opening direct to outside, 450 mm² of effective free area per MJ/h (Table 6.4.6.2). Effective means after louvre blades and insect mesh, so the louvre face is roughly twice the calculated area.
Mechanical0.3 L/s per MJ/h for forced draught burners (Table 6.4.9), delivered at low level, at or below burner level, with the Clause 6.4.10 interlock that senses actual air movement and fails safe.

Flue gas leaves the room too

The products going up the flue are room air. Counting them in the air balance is what keeps the room from going too negative once the hoods, the ovens and the doors are all working at once.

Who does what

The mechanical engineer sets the airflows, the openings, the position of the supply and the control logic. The licensed gasfitter installs and certifies the gas work, including flues and the isolation arrangement. Where an imported oven has no Australian certification, its approval route is a question for Building and Energy before it is connected, and that can take weeks, so raise it early.

Editions matter here: WA has required AS/NZS 5601.1:2022 for new installations since 30 March 2023, and the clause numbers moved between editions. Quote the edition with the clause.

Questions

When do gas ovens need extra ventilation air in a commercial kitchen?

For open flued appliances in a room, AS/NZS 5601.1:2022 Clause 6.4.6.3.1 divides gas input by room volume. Above 0.4 MJ/h per m3, additional ventilation is required: either a permanent opening direct to outside at 450 mm2 of effective free area per MJ/h, or a mechanical supply at low level with an interlock that shuts the gas if the air supply fails. The 3 MJ/h per m3 figure often quoted is the flueless limit, not the open flued one.

Does the make-up air fan have to be interlocked with the gas?

Where the mechanical supply is the pathway the appliances rely on for air, yes. AS/NZS 5601.1:2022 Clause 6.4.10 requires an interlock that senses actual air movement and fails safe, so the gas shuts off when the supply fails. The engineer specifies the logic and the licensed gasfitter installs and certifies the gas side.
